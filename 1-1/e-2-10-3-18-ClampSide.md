# The side of the clamp at the screen's edge

At the end of the scroll handler the game calls the routine of the player's offscreen bits (`ChkPOffscr`). It reads the screen's right edge, then its left edge, and his pixel. From the bits it finds, it calls [the clamp at the screen's edge](e-2-10-3-4-ScreenClamp.md) with `Y = 0` (the left edge) or `Y = 1` (the right edge), or it does not call it. [The clamp](e-2-10-3-4-ScreenClamp.md) and [the scroll's rule](e-2-10-3-12-ScrollRule.md) follow the clamp and the scroll, but not this choice. Here is the choice.

**Claim (round 30).** Take a real machine R at the routine's start (`ClampSide.places.chk`, with `Good`). Write

```
pix16(m)     = 256·page + pixel of his position
edge16(t, m) = 256·page + pixel of the screen's left edge (t = 0) or right edge (t = 1)
D_t(R)       = (edge16(t, R) − pix16(R)) mod 2¹⁶                     the difference that the code finds for the edge t
selM(R)      = selOf(bits(1, D_1))          if bits(1, D_1) ≠ 0
               selOf(bits(0, D_0))          else                    ∈ {none, some 0, some 1}
```

`bits(t, D)` is the byte of offscreen bits that the code finds from the high byte and the low byte of `D` (the tables are read from the ROM: `ClampSide.xOf`, `divX`, `bitsOf`); `selOf(b)` is `some 0` when bit 7 of `b` is set, `some 1` when the routine's mask is set, else `none`.

1. `chk_run`: after some steps in the frame the machine is at the clamp's entry with `Y = t` when `selM(R) = some t`, and at the scroll handler's return instruction when `selM(R) = none`. The stack, his position (pixel, page, fraction), the two edges and the two stack bytes above the stack (the scroll handler's return address) are those of R.
2. `clamp_end`: after some steps in the frame the machine R' is at the scroll handler's return instruction, with the stack, the two edges, the fraction and the two stack bytes above of R, and

```
pix16(R') = pix16(R)                                   if selM(R) = none
pix16(R') = (edge16(t, R) − amount(t)) mod 2¹⁶          if selM(R) = some t
```

`amount(t)` is the clamp's amount for `Y = t` (read from the ROM; 0 px for the left edge, 16 px for the right edge).

3. `left_after`: if moreover the right edge is 255 px right of the left edge,

```
edge16(1, R) ≡ edge16(0, R) + 255   (mod 2¹⁶)
```

then at the scroll handler's return instruction

```
(pix16(R') − edge16(0, R)) mod 2¹⁶ < 256
```

In words: the clamp leaves him at the screen's left edge or at most 255 px right of it (counted modulo 2¹⁶). It puts him at the left edge when he is at it or left of it, or beyond the right edge; at the right edge minus 16 px when he is in the last 16 px before the right edge; and else it leaves him where he is. With `d = D_1` (the right edge minus his pixel), the check shows on this ROM: `d < 16` the right edge, `16 ≤ d < 255` no clamp, `d ≥ 255` the left edge.

These are `ClampSide.chk_run`, `ClampSide.clamp_end` and `ClampSide.left_after` (axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; `left_of` is the pixel part of `left_after`, from `clamp_end`'s result), with the checks on the ROM `ClampSide.checkAll` and `ScreenClamp.check` as hypotheses. The private proof checks `checkAll` (`clamp_side_check`, generated by `genClampSide`, a few seconds natively).

**Proof.** By pieces of the ROM's code, as [the clamp](e-2-10-3-4-ScreenClamp.md) and [the scroll's rule](e-2-10-3-12-ScrollRule.md). The addresses come from the program: the routine of the offscreen bits is the target of the call at the routine's start, and its loop, the load of the edge's page, the call of the divide and the load of the bits are at fixed offsets in it, which the pieces check. For each piece the check walks the code for every value of the bytes it compares:

| piece | from | to | known values |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jCheck` | the routine's start | the loop, with `Y = 1` and `X = 0` | the stack byte (256): the return address on the stack, the saved index 0 |
| `s1Check` | the loop | the load of the edge's page | `Y`, the edge's pixel and his pixel (2 × 65,536): the low byte of the difference and its carry |
| `s2Check` | the load of the edge's page | the load of the bits, or the divide | `Y`, the edge's page, his page and the carry (2 × 131,072): the high byte of the difference decides; `X` is the table's offset |
| `s3Check` | the divide (a call) | the load of the bits | `Y`, the low difference and the stack byte (2 × 65,536): `X` from the low difference |
| `s4Check` | the load of the bits | the loop with `Y = 0` (bits 0 at the right edge), or the routine's end: the clamp's entry with its `Y`, or the scroll handler's return instruction | `Y`, `X` below 16 and the stack byte (2 × 4,096) |
| `cpX`, `cpP`, `cpT` | the clamp's entry | its return instruction | as `ScreenClamp`, and they also keep the two edges |
| `relCheck` | | | the selector for every `d < 2¹⁶` with the left edge 255 px left of the right edge: no clamp only when `d ≤ 255`; the left clamp's amount is 0 |

`side_run` chains the pieces of one edge, `fin_run` and `loop_run` the end of the loop, and `chk_run` the whole routine. `lo_hi` shows that the code's two bytes are those of the 16-bit difference, so `selM` is `selD` of `D_1` when the right edge is 255 px right of the left edge (`selM_rel`). `clamp_run` is the clamp of `ScreenClamp.run` with the edges kept.

**On the route (round 30).** `OnFrames.nearStepAt` uses `left_after`: the old field `nearStep` ([1-1-e-2-10-3-17](e-2-10-3-17-SideTimer.md)) is a theorem. The right edge's relation is the new field `rightRel`: at the routine's start in each counted frame but the last before the touch, the right edge is the left edge plus 255 px modulo 2¹⁶ and the left edge's page is not 255. The rest of the frame after the scroll handler's return instruction is the new field `endKeep`: when the frame ends with the side collision's timer at most 1, the scroll lock 0 and his place on the screen at least the threshold, his pixel at the end is that of the return instruction when the frame calls the push or the clamp after the scroll handler (on the other frames this is a theorem: `OnFrames.stillAt` keeps the position, and the routine chooses no clamp, `chk_run`), and the place byte is pixel − left edge modulo 256 (that the left edge at the end is that of the return instruction is a theorem: the return goes to the instruction after the call, `ret_step`, and from there the walk of `EdgeStep.keep_r` keeps the edge). Then `NearM` holds at the frame's end, with no condition at the frame's start. `numbersCheck` checks `checkAll` and that the threshold is at least 2 px. `endKeep` is not weaker than `nearStep`: it asks the exact pixel and place byte where `nearStep` asked only the bound of 2 px.

**What is not proved here.** `rightRel`: it needs walks that follow the right edge's two bytes from the frame's start to the scroll handler and on the frames not counted, the routine in the scroll that sets the right edge from the left edge, and the state at control. `endKeep`: after the call only the pushes move him; a frame that ends with the side collision's timer at most 1 has no push after the call (round 29: a walk of the timer loses it at 18 stores through an index or a pointer); and the drawing at the frame's end writes his place on the screen as pixel − left edge modulo 256 (it is skipped on every other frame while the injury timer runs). `Moves.stop` needs the edge at the frame's start: on its measured frames the left clamp puts him at P(Q), so it asks that the frame starts at or right of the left edge, or 1 px left of it with a small speed.
