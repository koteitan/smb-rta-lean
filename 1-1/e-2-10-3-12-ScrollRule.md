# The scroll's rule

**Claim (round 25).** Take a real machine R at the entry of the scroll handler (the first call at the end of the player's routine, `PlayerSubs`), with `Good`. Then after some steps in the frame it is at the call of the clamp at the screen's edge (`ChkPOffscr`), and:

```
s(R') = s(R)                                     the stack
pixel, page, fraction of the position: kept
left16(R') = left16(R)
  ∨ ( Allows(R) ∧ ∃ Y, Y ≤ X(R) ∧ left16(R') = (left16(R) + Y) mod 2¹⁶ )

left16(m)  = 256·page + pixel of the screen's left edge, at the two operands of the clamp (ScreenClamp.places)
X(m)       = the scroll's amount byte + the platforms' force on the scroll, a byte (amountOf)
Allows(m)  = scroll lock = 0 ∧ his place on the screen ≥ thr1 ∧ side collision's timer = 0 ∧ 1 ≤ X(m) ≤ 128
thr1       = the threshold of the code's first test of his place (read from the program; 80 px)
```

This is `ScrollRule.scroll_rule` (axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`), with the check on the ROM `ScrollRule.check` as its hypothesis. It also keeps the two bytes above the stack (the return address).

**The whole call.** From a real machine Q at the call (`PlayerSubs`, with `Good`) to the instruction after the call (`PlayerSubs + 3`), with all values read at Q (`scroll_call`, `scroll_edge`):

```
s(R') = s(Q)
edge(R') = edge(Q)  ∨  ( Allows(Q) ∧ ∃ Y, Y ≤ X(Q) ∧ edge(R') ≤ edge(Q) + 256·Y )        edge = SurfaceRead.edgeOf = 256·left16 + 255 (edgeOf_left16)
```

After the scroll, the clamp at the screen's edge and the routine of the offscreen bits do not write the edge's two bytes, and the scroll handler returns. The private proof checks it (`scroll_rule_check`, generated by `genScrollRule`, about 3 s natively, with the file's load).

In words: the scroll moves the left edge by at most the scroll's amount, and only when the four bytes allow it. Before the clamp nothing else moves the edge or the position. The amount is the move of the frame in whole pixels (the move routine stores it) plus the platforms' force.

**Proof.** By pieces of the ROM's code, as [the clamp](e-2-10-3-4-ScreenClamp.md). The addresses come from the program: the scroll handler is the target of the call at `PlayerSubs`, and its loads (`LDA`, `LDY` absolute), branches and the final jump give the cuts and the bytes (`ScrollRule.places`). The edge's two bytes are the clamp's operands. For each piece the check walks the code for every value of the byte it branches on:

| piece | from | to | known values |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amountCheck` | the entry | the load of the scroll lock | the amount byte and the platforms' force (65,536 pairs): the amount is their sum |
| `lockCheck` | the load of the scroll lock | his place on the screen, or no scroll | the scroll lock (256) |
| `pfsCheck` | his place on the screen | the timer, or no scroll | his place (256): at least `thr1` |
| `sideCheck` | the timer | the amount, or no scroll | the timer (256) |
| `ldyCheck` | the amount | the second test of his place, with Y known, or no scroll | the amount (256): `1 ≤ X ≤ 128` (the decrement is not negative); Y = X, or X − 1 (by the code's test of the amount) |
| `nearCheck` | the second test of his place | the scroll, or the load of the amount again | Y and his place (65,536) |
| `ldy2Check` | the load of the amount again | the scroll, with Y = X | the amount (256) |
| `scrCheck` | the scroll | the load of the edge's page | Y and the edge's pixel (65,536): the pixel + Y, and the carry |
| `pgCheck` | the load of the edge's page | the clamp's call | the stack byte, the page and the carry (131,072): the page + the carry; the call that sets the screen's right edge returns |
| `initCheck` | no scroll | the clamp's call | none |
| `jsrCheck` | the call at `PlayerSubs` | the scroll handler's entry | the stack byte (256): the return address on the stack |
| `tailCheck` | the clamp's call | the instruction after the call | the stack byte (256) and the return address: the edge's two bytes are not written |

Each piece keeps the stack (the call inside `pgCheck` returns) and does not write the position's three bytes; the pieces before the scroll do not write the edge's bytes, and those after it keep the other byte of the edge. `scroll_rule` chains them (`Seg`, `Seg.trans`) and adds the pixel and the page with the carry (`left_add`); `scroll_call` adds the call and the rest of the scroll handler. That `Y ≤ X` is `xOK_range` and `dec_toNat`. The thresholds and the constant of the test of the amount are read from the program (`Places.thr1`, `thr2`, `two`), not written in the file; so `Y` may be 0 in the statement (then the edge does not move).

**What is not proved here.** This does not prove the field `edge` ([1-1-e-2-10-3-11](e-2-10-3-11-EdgeStart.md)). For it, three more things are needed: that the edge's two bytes are kept from the frame's start to the call and from the instruction after the call to the frame's end (walks of the whole frame that follow the two bytes), and that the scroll is allowed only when he is far enough right of the edge (his place on the screen is written in the frame before, so this is a relation of two frames). `scroll_rule` is not used by the route yet. No field changes.

**Measured** (`edge-real FM2 256`, round 25; the table is on [1-1-e-2-10-3-11](e-2-10-3-11-EdgeStart.md)): the edge never moved more than 3 px in a frame, and never more than his move in that frame; on the 11,289 frames where it moved (of 137,955), he was at least 81 px right of it at the scroll handler, and at least 79 px right of it at the frame's end.

**File:** `Theorems/ScrollRule.lean`. `Main.lean`: the generator `genScrollRule` (in `gen-proofs`).
